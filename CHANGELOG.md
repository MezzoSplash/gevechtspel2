# Changelog

Release notes, newest first. Downloads are on the [Releases page](https://github.com/MezzoSplash/gevechtspel2/releases).

**v0.6.1** — Netcode die hoge ping verdraagt, en drie dingen die niet deden wat ze moesten.

Met een hoge ping of packet loss zweven en schuiven andere spelers niet meer. Elke pose komt met de zendtijd van de afzender binnen en wordt over een buffer geïnterpoleerd, dus een schokkerige verbinding geeft geen pawns meer die vooruit razen, stil blijven staan of de lucht in hangen. Er zit een klein netwerkpaneel in de HUD (ping, packet loss, buffer) dat je met een schakelaar aanzet.

Bots kruipen niet meer de hele wedstrijd over de kaart. Een duik werd nooit afgesloten, dus een bot die één keer dook bleef gecrouched lopen tot hij doodging. Ze duiken en rollen nu weer en lopen gewoon rechtop.

De shank raakt weer. De lunge liep altijd de volle 2,5 m, ook als de tegenstander vlak voor je stond, en de treffer werd vanaf het eind van die stap bepaald: je stak dus voorbij alles wat dichterbij stond. De steek kiest nu eerst een doel, de stap stopt vlak ervoor, de treffer telt langs het hele pad en de kegel is even ruim als die van de bash (40° in plaats van 28°). Sprintend raak je van dichtbij tot ongeveer 4,5 m.

Intern: de killcam is opgesplitst in kleinere stukken (#317, #368), er is een vaste replay-testrun per kaart (#250, #369), de MVP-killcam gaat naar de grootste combo (#358) en de bouwversie staat naast het protocol in het menu en de serverlijst (#356).

**Niet compatibel met v0.6.0** (NET_VERSION 0.5.21): poses dragen nu de zendtijd van de afzender en de pose van een bot bevat ook zijn slide, dus een 0.6.0-speler wordt geweigerd. Werk server en clients samen bij.

**v0.6.0** — The guns have real models now.

The knife, rifle, LMG, pistol, revolver, shotgun, slug and sniper are proper models with their own fire animation and their own muzzle flash, instead of the low-poly placeholders. Tracers leave from the muzzle of each model, and the flash shows in the killcam too. The SMG already had its model.

The frag is a crystal: a cut olive gem with a fuse band that tumbles through the air, trails a thin streak when thrown hard, and pulses faster and turns red in the last 0.4 s before it goes off. In team modes its cap glows in the thrower's team colour. The blast bursts into crystal splinters with a ring that runs out to the edge of the splash radius, so you can see how far it reaches.

The game starts on the G2 logo instead of the Godot logo, and the logo is the window and taskbar icon.

The server's position corrections reach players again. When the server refused a jump it could not believe, or pulled someone out of the void, the correction never arrived, so a player could stay where the server said they were not. Under the hood the biggest scripts were split into smaller pieces; nothing should play differently because of that.

**Compatible with v0.5.20** (NET_VERSION stays 0.5.20): nothing new goes over the wire, so 0.5.20 and 0.6.0 play together. A 0.5.20 server still sends no corrections; update the server to get them.

**v0.5.20** — A grapple, and two maps rebuilt for the way the game moves now.

Hold the grapple key (V or Mouse 4, rebindable) and a rope shoots from your left hand to whatever you aim at, up to 32 m away, and pulls you there hard: up to 22 m/s, upward too. Let go and you keep every bit of that speed, so the release is the move: grapple into a wallrun, off a roof into a dive, across a gap into a surf. It ends when you let go, arrive, lose the line or wallrun or dive, and it is ready again 3 s after you fire it. Everyone sees your rope and your reaching hand. A kill on the rope or just after letting go is a GRAPPLE KILL, and a pull counts as a combo move.

Foundry is a real foundry now. A casting hall stands in the middle with a cupola furnace whose stack rises through the skylight; the hall roof is high ground you reach by ramp or grapple, and the furnace splits it in two. Each team has an ore bunker by its dock with a long surf down the outer wall into the other team's back lane, two speed strips on the way. Ladles hang from two crane runways to grapple to, and the yard is containers, a shed, the water tank, a sand heap, ingots and moulds instead of loose crates.

Townhouses is a Dutch canal street. A canal runs down the middle, three metres below the quays: wallrun the quay walls, take the steps down, hide behind the boats. Each team's row of canal houses has a steep tiled roof that is a surf from your own end to past the middle, with two strips; a ramp up the back alley takes you onto it. A drawbridge under a tall steel portal is the middle of the map and the anchor everyone grapples to.

A new class: Shank. A knife as your primary, a pistol and one frag. Holding the knife you sprint about 14 m/s; a stab is 50 from the front and 100 from behind, and at sprint on the ground a stab lunges you 2.5 m forward. Bots can draw it too. In Gun Game a shank kill sets the victim back, like the bash.

The crouch is a squat now instead of a torso sticking out behind the legs. Bots take the wallrun routes on all maps and actually run the wall when they take one. The main menu plays "Reload", a new song made for the game.

**Not compatible with v0.5.19** (NET_VERSION 0.5.20): the grapple rope is synced, Foundry and Townhouses have new collision and the shank is a new weapon, so a 0.5.19 peer is refused. Update server and clients together.

**v0.5.19** — The UI is cut from the same crystal as the characters. Every menu piece, button and HUD slot is a shard now: a rectangle with four unequal chamfered corners, flat facets and a glowing seam along one edge that carries the meaning (team colour on scores and the kill feed, ice on what is selected or ready). Smoke glass is the idle material, pale shell is what you have selected or are holding, and the old amber is gone; gold stays for trick rewards only.

A speed meter sits right above your health: your horizontal speed as a big number, with the shard's bottom seam filling up to 24 m/s and a notch at sprint speed, so you see at a glance whether a carry, dive or surf is paying off. Past sprint the number turns ice, and a small chip names the move you are in (WALLRUN, DIVE, SLIDE or SURF). It hides while you are dead and in the final killcam. The combo meter moves up when the stack under it would cover it, which already happened at 720p.

The screens follow the new look, not only the colours. The main menu has a wordmark with a crystal mark, a hint inside each button and a red seam on Quit. The pause menu shows the score as three shards around the clock (team colours in team modes, you against the leader in free-for-all) above a centred column of buttons. Settings show the open tab's name as the page title, put all six tabs in one row, and give the sliders a glowing fill and a diamond grabber. Kill feed rows sit on a shard with the killer's colour on the edge.

Your resolution stays put with two monitors. The game used to measure the saved size against the monitor its window started on and save the result, so with a 1080p primary and a 1440p monitor to play on, a 2560×1440 pick became 1920×1080 on every launch. A pick is now only replaced when no connected monitor can show it.

**v0.5.18** — Bots have characters now. Every bot in a match draws one of four from `data/bots/*.tres` in a fixed per-slot cycle — aggressive (closes to about 9 m, slides in from 20 m, breaks off only under 25 HP, throws frags), holder (keeps about 13.6 m, wide tolerance, never throws a frag, breaks off under 55 HP), flanker (will not open fire from the front: it circles for an angle of at least ~70° or comes to 2.5 m point blank, and stops circling after 2.5 s of patience) and chaos (the old numbers). Difficulty still sets how sharp a bot is; the character sets how it fights. Measured mean fighting distance over 10 s: aggressive 9.35 m, chaos 10.92 m, holder 13.63 m.

Bots find and run wallrun walls on their own. Detection is raycast-only, so new or moved walls work without per-map data: a flat face at least 7 m long along the run line, at least 2 m tall, with walkable floor under it and clear space along the run. A bot jumps in at 35°, lets the shared wallrun code run it, and wall-jumps off at 0.9 s or as soon as its target is in sight — never while fighting, sliding, diving or unsticking. The chance per met wall comes from the difficulty (Easy 0, Medium 0.3, Hard 0.7). The old bot wallrun also never applied the wall's gravity sink; it does now, so a bot's run looks like a player's.

Bots follow route hints generated from the map. `data/bot_routes/skyline.json` (43 routes) and `rooftops.json` (59) come from `tools/gen_bot_routes`, which bakes the map and asks the same wall finder the bots use; every route is validated once at load, and a line through world geometry or a point without walkable floor under it is dropped with a warning, so a moved wall can never steer a bot into it. During a hunt a bot takes the longest route that starts within 3 m and brings it closer, presses crouch once on a slide route, and falls back to the normal hunt when there is none; a route without progress for 0.6 s is abandoned.

Under the hood the bot code was restructured for this: state is one `State` enum plus a separate `Route` axis with a single writer (#268, no behaviour change), the pawn roster is built once per physics tick and each bot caches line of sight for 0.1 s (#272: 53–67% fewer rays, and an enemy behind a wall stays visible at most one tick longer). New checks: `check_bot_style`, `check_bot_state`, `check_bot_perf`, `check_bot_wall`, `check_bot_route`.

A Hard bot with an empty gun runs for cover instead of reloading in the open. It picks a spot within 8 m that the enemy cannot see, once, and sprints there; if nothing nearby blocks the shot it runs straight away with its back to them. Easy and Medium still reload where they stand. With one round left and the enemy inside 6 m, Hard closes for a bash instead of starting that reload. Nothing new goes over the wire.

The viewmodel spring no longer throws the gun off screen when a frame runs long. It still takes one step at 60 fps; a longer frame is split into 60 fps steps, and the sideways sway is clamped the way the landing dip already was.

A bot no longer lobs a frag into a ceiling or a door head. The toss is refused when the arc hits the world, the cooldown is not spent, and one higher pitch is tried before giving up. A player's own throw is unchanged.

Falling out of a map sends you back. Every map has a kill height (Skyline stays at -3, the other four at -4, under the floor). Under it you die and respawn on the map, except in the first second after a spawn: then you are put back on that spawn instead of dying twice. The rest of spawn protection no longer swallows that fall, so after the first second you always die and come back. An LOS bash on a crouched target no longer snaps your body through the floor. The landing has to fit a standing capsule and a crouched one, with floor under the feet; otherwise the body stays put.

**Compatible with v0.5.17** (NET_VERSION stays 0.5.16): nothing new goes over the wire — bots are server pawns and the route hints are map data the server reads — so a 0.5.17 client plays on a 0.5.18 server and a 0.5.18 client next to a 0.5.17 one. Everything that changes how a match plays is server-side, so updating the dedicated server is enough to feel it; the viewmodel spring is the one fix a client needs.

**v0.5.17** — Bots only know what they can notice. Below Hard, a bot no longer sees every enemy through the walls: it knows someone it has seen, someone who shot it, or a shot it heard within its hearing range (Easy 10 m, Medium 22 m), it walks to the last place it knew them for its memory (Easy 4 s, Medium 8 s), and when that spot is empty or the memory runs out it forgets them and patrols the navmesh instead of standing at the spawn. A heard shot is placed a few metres off, so a bot that only heard you comes looking near you, not at you. Hard keeps the old omniscience, so the top difficulty plays exactly as before. `NET_VERSION` stays 0.5.16: bots are server pawns, nothing new goes over the wire.

And a reload of another player plays the gun they actually hold: The index that rides `sync_reload` was the shooter's slot index (their class: primary/secondary) while the receiver read it as an armory index, so on a Breacher's screen everyone else's shotgun did the rifle's magazine reload, and a Runner's revolver did the magazine instead of the cylinder. The sender now puts `Weapon.reload_wire_index()` (the armory index) on the wire, the receiver falls back to the gun the pawn is holding when the index names no gun this build knows, and `check_reload_style_sync` walks every class and every slot on both ends of the contract. `NET_VERSION` stays 0.5.16: only the animation on another player's body changes, gameplay and protocol do not.

**Compatible with v0.5.16** (NET_VERSION stays 0.5.16): nothing new goes over the wire, so a 0.5.16 client can play on a 0.5.17 server and a 0.5.16 client next to a 0.5.17 one. The server is what changes the bots, so only the dedicated server has to be updated to see it. Under the hood the big files were split further (shared/style_track.gd, shared/hud_layout.gd, scripts/hud/notice_layer.gd) with no behaviour change, and three headless checks that used to pass only on their retry are deterministic now.

**v0.5.16** — A landing roll can no longer be cut by a jump: the first `ROLL_JUMP_LOCK` (0.3 s) of the roll refuses it and holds the press in the jump buffer, and jumping out of the roll drops the horizontal speed to sprint instead of carrying the dive's 15 m/s, while rolling the roll out still pays `ROLL_CARRY`. Slide-dive and wall jump are unchanged. No protocol change.

A carry can be steered now. At surf or strip speed the old code only added speed along the wish direction, so the forward speed stayed and a held key bent the path sideways: it felt like ice. The velocity is turned toward the wish direction at `CARRY_TURN_RATE` (7 rad/s) with its speed kept, holding the opposite way brakes on `CARRY_COUNTER_FRICTION` instead of turning, and the last `CARRY_FADE` (0.6 s) of the `CARRY_TIME` 2.5 s ramps the friction back up to ground friction while accelerate refills toward wish speed in the same tick — so a carry you run out hands over at sprint and never dumps you at walking speed (playtest). A landing roll steers the same way at `ROLL_TURN_RATE` (3.5 rad/s). Local movement only, no protocol change.

Reloading looks like the gun you hold. Each weapon names a style in its `.tres` — mag (rifle, SMG, pistol, LMG), shells (shotgun, slug), cylinder (revolver) or bolt (sniper) — and `scripts/reload_anims.gd` plays it as keyframed curves on the reload's own timing: the mag style rolls the gun 35° on its side, drops the spent magazine, seats a fresh one and pulls the charging handle (the LMG hinges its feed cover open first); shells loads one round per missing shell and ends with a pump; the revolver rolls 70° over, flicks the cases out and closes with a spin; the sniper comes up, works the bolt and seats a clip. The magazine, shell and cover are procedural crystal blocks and the left hand is the first-person crystal hand, so no new assets. The reload timing and the ammo are unchanged, and this half is first person only: the third-person hand and the sync to other players come next.

And everyone else sees it. A reload start is synced on the cosmetic channel, so a reloading pawn's body plays the same style and pulls its left hand off the gun to the mag, and the killcam does the same for the ghost. **Not compatible with v0.5.15** (NET_VERSION 0.5.16): a 0.5.15 client shows no reload on anyone else, so everyone must update, including the dedicated server.

The final killcam is cut around the play. It no longer starts a fixed five seconds before the death: it starts at the beginning of the run that ends in the kill — a wallrun chain, a dive, a slide — and fast-forwards the stretches where nothing happens behind a VHS speed-up look (scanlines, a rolling tracking band, tape noise, a `>> SP` camcorder marker), so the replay is the combo and not the walk to it. Only events the killer or victim is part of count as action, so a busy bot match does not glue the whole clip together. A kill with nothing behind it is cut to two and a half seconds instead of shown in full, and the replay is fitted to the wall clock: earlier footage fills the time as tape and the rest stretches the kill's slow motion, so there is no long freeze at the end — and a long chain is sped up to fit instead of being cut off before the kill, which used to happen when the server unlocked while the replay was still running. The combo payout climbs in the top bar and lands exactly on the shot, the kill gets a Tony Hawk "landed" sting on top of the hit sound (with the special sting for a big combo), and the clip ends holding the last frame for at most a beat. Local replay only, no protocol change.
